Privacy, plainly

lifeline keeps what you tell it so it can bring it back at the right time, and nothing else. It is run by one person for a small circle of up to 55 people. Nothing is sold, and there are no ads. Last updated 30 October 2026.

  • Yours, not ours. What you keep is used to help you. It is never sold, shared for marketing, or used to profile you.
  • Your yes first. Nothing that spends money, deletes something or reaches another person happens without you confirming it.
  • Secrets stay out. Passwords, seed phrases, private keys, API tokens and card numbers are removed before anything is saved.
  • Groups see nothing personal. Your reminders, health, money and memories never appear in a group chat.
  • Leave with everything. Say "export my data" for a full copy, or "delete my account" to erase it all.
  • History doesn't pile up. Chat history is deleted after 180 days. What you asked lifeline to keep stays until you delete it.

What it never keeps

  • Passwords and PINs. They are refused in plain words. A note on where to find things is fine.
  • Seed phrases and private keys. They are replaced with "[hidden seed phrase]" or "[hidden key]" before saving. A transaction hash is left alone.
  • API keys, tokens and card numbers. These are replaced before saving, too.

lifeline only ever reads crypto wallets. It never asks for a key and cannot sign anything.

Outside services

lifeline passes each service the minimum it needs:

  • AI model providers. lifeline runs on several, picked per task. Whichever one handles a message gets that message, the recent conversation, a short summary of earlier turns, confirmed memories, your name and time zone, to understand you and reply. It doesn't get your whole history. Each provider's own terms for developers cover what it does with what's sent; some free tiers may use it to improve their services.
  • Web search through those same providers. It gets the question, and your city when it matters. "Find my personal data online" sends your name and the details you choose to search for, so it can look.
  • Messaging. Telegram, text and iMessage providers (Twilio, httpSMS, TextBee, Sendblue or BlueBubbles), email (Resend or SMTP), and app notification services (Google, Mozilla, Apple, Microsoft). Each gets the message and the address it goes to.
  • Sign-in with Google or Apple: confirmation of who you are.
  • Lookups you ask for. A link to Google Safe Browsing. Public wallet addresses to GoPlus, Etherscan, OpenSea and Magic Eden. A city, country, team or flight number for weather, prayer times, holidays, sports and flights.
  • Flutterwave, only for a bill you said yes to: the biller, the amount and the account number for that bill.

The data is stored with Supabase (Postgres). The server runs on Railway and the website on Vercel, with secrets in Doppler.

Storage and security

  • Database. One Postgres database, reached only by the lifeline server, over an encrypted connection.
  • Backups. These follow the host's schedule, so a deleted record can stay in a backup until that copy expires.
  • Sign-in. Codes are stored hashed, expire quickly and are rate-limited. Google and Apple sign-ins use one-time checks.
  • Links. Picture and plan links are signed and expire. Plan pages are unlisted.
  • Reading the web. lifeline only fetches public addresses and checks every redirect.
  • On your phone. Inside Telegram's app, you can choose to keep your emergency card in Telegram's secure storage on your phone.

Health, safety and money

lifeline helps you remember and keep track. It is not a doctor, an emergency service, a lawyer or a financial adviser.

  • Medication. It reminds you of what you told it, and never suggests doses or changes.
  • Cycle. Predictions are estimates, not contraception.
  • Safety check-ins. These depend on your phone and the network, so use them as an extra layer, never your only plan.
  • Scam checks. A check that finds nothing never means safe.
  • Money tools. Debt plans, tax dates and rent timelines are arithmetic and reminders, not advice.
